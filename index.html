D01

Comparative Normal Anatomy & Physiology

A refresher across species before the disease-specific weeks begin — where dog, cat, horse, cow, and small ruminant anatomy converge, and where they don't.

CardiovascularFour-chambered heart layout; ruminant vs. monogastric conduction quirks; species differences in normal HR/RR.
GISimple-stomached vs. ruminant (four-compartment) digestion; equine hindgut fermentation.
RenalNephron structure; species differences in urine concentrating ability and normal USG ranges.
MusculoskeletalAxial vs. appendicular landmarks used in physical exam and imaging orientation.

Key information, worked example: Ruminants digest via a four-compartment stomach (reticulum, rumen, omasum, abomasum) with microbial fermentation upstream of the true stomach (abomasum) — this is why ruminant drug absorption, gas accumulation (bloat), and forestomach disease patterns differ fundamentally from a dog or cat's single-compartment stomach. The horse instead ferments in an enlarged hindgut (cecum/colon) downstream of a simple stomach, which is why equine colic and dietary carbohydrate overload present so differently from ruminant bloat despite both being "fermentation" problems.

Sample Q1 of 30

A ruminant with acute bloat is most likely to have gas trapped due to failure of which process?

  • A. Abomasal emptying
  • B. Eructation (belching) reflex
  • C. Rumen absorption of VFAs
  • D. Salivary buffering
Answer: B. Free-gas bloat results from failure to eructate (e.g., esophageal obstruction, vagal indigestion, or positional issues); frothy bloat instead results from stable foam preventing gas from coalescing enough to be eructated.
Sample Q2 of 30

Compared to the dog, the normal feline heart is best characterized on thoracic radiographs by:

  • A. A more horizontal cardiac axis
  • B. A more vertical, "valentine-shaped" cardiac silhouette
  • C. A markedly larger cardiac silhouette relative to body size
  • D. Absence of a visible cranial waist
Answer: B. The feline heart typically sits more vertically in the thorax than the dog's, producing the classic "valentine" silhouette on the VD/DV view — a normal-variant detail worth recognizing before pattern-matching cardiomegaly.
